Saw Tidewater tonight. There's an eleven-minute single take of a woman mending a net while the tide comes in. Nothing "happens." I couldn't look away.

Honestly? Long takes ask for patience, and they pay it back in attention. By minute six I was noticing the gulls, the rope fibres, the way her hands slowed as the light went.

The thing is, a cut tells you where to look. A long take lets you choose. That freedom is the whole point of slow cinema for me — the film trusts me to find my own way in.
