title: Why slow cinema matters to me
fictional: true

I've been watching a lot of slow films this year, and honestly? They've changed how I watch everything.

The thing is, fast editing trains us to wait for the next cut. Slow cinema untrains that. After Tidewater and The Quiet Harvest, I notice framing, sound, and light in ordinary films I'd never have noticed before.

But slowness isn't a virtue on its own — Seven Bells at Dusk taught me that. A long take has to hold something: a task, a change, a feeling building. When it does, it's the most respectful thing a film can do. It trusts you.
