title: Seven Bells at Dusk — a long take done wrong
fictional: true
---
Seven Bells at Dusk has long takes, but honestly? They felt empty.

The thing is, length alone isn't the point. In Tidewater the long take holds a task, a change, a slow transformation. Here the camera just sits on a hallway while nothing evolves — no light shifting, no gesture building. It felt like the film was showing off its patience rather than using it.

Slow cinema is a contract: I give you time, you give me attention back. Seven Bells took the time and gave very little in return (I checked my watch twice, which never happens).
