title: Why cozy games work for me
fictional: true

Moss & Kettle, Lighthouse Bakery, Paper Gardens. Honestly? These three carried me through a stressful spring.

The thing is, comfort games aren't escapism in a lazy sense. They give you a small world that responds kindly to effort. When the real world feels unfair, a place where care is always rewarded is genuinely restorative.

I used to separate "real" games from cozy ones. Now I think they're answering different questions. Ashen Vigil asks: can you improve? Moss & Kettle asks: can you rest? Both questions matter (some weeks, the second one more).
